### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's thesis is that order dependence is a real defect of incremental face clustering for personal libraries and that a provably order-invariant incremental clusterer fixes it without cost. A sceptic would answer as follows. First, the need is never shown. Real imports are close to chronological, not random shuffles; nothing measures how often a user re-imports, or whether a 2.5-point mean F1 spread is visible to anyone. Second, an order-free method already exists in the paper: average-linkage agglomerative clustering. It has zero order spread, beats the proposed method by 2 F1 points on CALFW and 10 on CPLFW, and on libraries of 389-1,421 faces re-running it costs very little. The only argument left against it is speed, and that rests on one 13k-face LFW stream run with outdated thresholds. Third, the method itself is much less new than it sounds. At the shipped m=1, almost every face that could link is "core", so the clusterer is essentially single-linkage connected components at tau_l plus nearest-neighbour attachment and a centroid merge. Connected components are order-invariant for trivial reasons, so the proposition is correct but shallow. Fourth, the robustness gains are confounded by unequal tuning. The proposed method was grid-searched over four parameters on all seven degraded conditions, while the baselines used thresholds from a clean split or fixed legacy values. Fifth, the paper's own data contradict its causal story about failures. It says the pose and age errors come from the embedding, yet agglomerative clustering on the same MBF embeddings recovers CPLFW to 0.881. Even the EdgeFace-S "upgrade" lifts CPLFW verification by only +0.32 points (CI includes zero). Of its +7.1 clustering points, about 5 appear only after a threshold-grid shift that MBF never received. On this reading, Cove ships a faster recogniser and a stricter, better-searched threshold set, and the order-invariance story is incidental.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Agglomerative (or HDBSCAN) re-run per import at personal-library scale. It is order-free, more accurate on pose and age, and probably fast enough below a few thousand faces. The paper does not show that the incremental path is needed at the scales it evaluates.
2. Threshold search effort as the source of the EdgeFace-S gain. An equally wide, unshifted-plus-shifted grid for MBF, or a scale-normalised threshold such as percentile-of-random-pair similarity applied to both models, would separate the model effect from the tuning effect.
3. Tuning on degraded conditions as the source of the robustness gains. Re-tuning First-Leader, Two-stage and Agglomerative on the same seed-1 degraded libraries would test whether attach/link separation, rather than the tuning protocol, drives Table II.
4. Order canonicalisation of the leader algorithm (sort faces by a deterministic key such as capture time or a quality score before each re-cluster) as a cheap way to get reproducibility without a new clusterer.
5. Agglomerative clustering on EdgeFace-S embeddings. If it again beats ours on CPLFW, the recogniser switch has not closed the pose gap that motivated it.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- End users of photo managers, whose perceived harm from order dependence, and whose tolerance for splits versus merges, is never measured.
- People who appear in photos but do not own the library (strangers, children), whose embeddings are created and stored without their say.
- Users of self-hosted managers (Immich, PhotoPrism) as a comparison population; their density-based clustering is the real competitor but is not evaluated.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that order invariance under arbitrary permutations is the stability property users care about. The behaviour users actually see is that people they have already named should not split, merge or reshuffle as new photos arrive. That is monotonic or temporal stability under growth, which order invariance neither implies nor guarantees, and the paper's evidence on it (m4) uses a superseded configuration.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The proof sketch for set-determinism is sound at the stated level, and the experiments confirm zero order spread for every configuration tested.
- The paper is unusually candid about failure modes (occlusion, CALFW, CPLFW, grid-edge thresholds, shared identity pool), and all numbers checked against the JSON results matched the manuscript tables to three decimals.
- The negative-result section (Sec. V-C: re-tuning and common-neighbour gating both fail on held-out data) is a genuine strength and guards against the over-tuning concern in M3 for the proposed method itself.
- Crop-identical recogniser comparison with bootstrap CIs (Table III) is good practice for a CPU-deployment paper.
