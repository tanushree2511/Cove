## B. Findings (all MINOR)

**M1 L313 "14 to 69 times at 8,290 (30 and 20 s against 0.44 and 1.25 s)".** The two quoted pairs give 69 (30.3/0.44) and 16 (19.6/1.25), not 14. The 14 is SciPy 17.4 s / 1.25 s (13.95), a number absent from the parenthesis. A reader dividing the quoted numbers gets 16 to 69. Fix: "16 to 69 times (30 and 20 s against 0.44 and 1.25 s), 14 times against SciPy (17 s)".

**M2 L312-314 what the baseline timing contains.** In exp29 (T.c_avg_decomposed) the baseline time includes the dense all-pairs cosine pass over all faces (2.5e9 pairs at 49,740), the components and the linkage, i.e. a batch re-run from raw embeddings. Our time is labels() after the index exists (the 316 s build is reported separately). That is the right framing for "re-run on each import vs incremental read", but the text does not say the baseline includes the similarity pass, and calls SciPy "a lower bound" for it. From scratch at 49,740 faces ours is 316 + 15 = 331 s against 234 s (c-avg) and 174 s (SciPy). Fix: add "baseline times include their all-pairs similarity pass; from scratch at 49,740 faces our index build plus read is slower than both".

**M3 L191 "Ours gave identical partitions in every order and batch split".** In the Table I runs (exp14 _test -> stable with batch=None) ours is added in one batch in 3 shuffled orders per library (min ARI exactly 1.0 over the 240 pairs). Batch splits are covered by unit tests on synthetic data (batch sizes 1, 7, 13, 17, 50; cove/tests/test_stable_cluster.py) and by the two-device runs, not by the 240-pair table. Fix: "in 3 shuffled orders on every test library (batch splits: unit tests)".

**M4 L35-36 abstract device agreement and L224-227 omitted conditions (carry-overs from round 13).** (a) The abstract says "By construction ... identical people on two devices" without "given identical embeddings"; the body (L319) and the proposition (exact arithmetic) carry the qualifier, and exp21 recomputes both replicas in one process from the same embeddings. (b) The average-linkage comparison still omits mixed (-0.0025, [-0.0060,+0.0002]) and look-alike (+0.0037, [0.0000,+0.0104]); both are inside the eight-condition mean (Table I caption), so nothing is hidden. Fix: add the qualifier to the abstract; add the two conditions to L224-225.

## E. Strongest counter-argument
The paper's own numbers say the clusterer buys little over a simpler design. Without corrections it ties average linkage (0.9854 vs 0.9857), is 7 points worse on profile photos and, with MBF, 13 points worse on CPLFW. With corrections it is within 0.003 F1 of constrained average linkage and is the least tolerant of wrong clicks (its gain is gone by p=0.05). The distinctive-sounding properties (device agreement, exact retraction, freedom to revise) are conceded to follow from any recompute-from-sets design. What remains is the speed claim, measured on rotated copies of one 8,290-face set against a self-written Python baseline whose time includes the all-pairs pass while ours excludes a 316 s index build. A hostile reviewer can call the contribution a sound but unproven engineering recipe. The defence is in the text: it is labelled an application-and-measurement study, reports its losses, and bounds claims to simulation.

## F. Caveats worth one sentence each (not defects)
1. The Two-stage baseline's tuned leader threshold is 0.70, the upper edge of both the 0.05 and 0.01 grids, and its tuning ARI (0.9853) exceeds ours (0.9822); it may be truncated, but it is order-dependent and not the strongest baseline, so no claim hinges on it.
2. exp19b shows the frozen rule is itself order-dependent (order agreement 0.995 EdgeFace-S, 0.988 MBF), which supports the paper.
3. In the deletion test the corrections are binding before deletion; after deleting 30% only 2-10 remain and their binding was not re-checked.
