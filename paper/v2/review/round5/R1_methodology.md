# Round 5 - Reviewer 1 (Methodology)

Manuscript: paper/v2/cove_paper.tex. Audited against evaluation/results/*.json, exp14-exp30 scripts, stable_cluster.py (incl. remove(), _labels_constrained), and by running evaluation/verify_paper_numbers.py (129 ok, 2 mismatch).

## Recommendation
**Minor revision (borderline major on claim sizing in three places).** Confidence 4/5. Every printed number I could trace matches the result files (details at the end). The weaknesses are not arithmetic but (a) a headline claim ("beneficial up to one wrong click in twenty") that the paper's own sweep does not support, (b) abstract wording wider than the evidence, (c) consequence results that are guaranteed by construction or depend on author-designed baselines, and (d) timing and statistics hygiene.

## Issues

**1. MAJOR - "beneficial up to about one wrong click in twenty" contradicts the sweep.**
Locations: Abstract ("Corrections stay beneficial up to about one wrong click in twenty"), Sec. VI-B ("+0.0009 at 0.05 ... (intervals include zero)"), Discussion, Conclusion ("Corrections remain beneficial up to about one wrong click in twenty").
Evidence: exp25_effort_edgeface_s.json, ours gain over discarding: p=0.02 +0.0034 [0.0015,0.0054]; p=0.05 +0.0009 [-0.0011,+0.0029]; p=0.10 +0.0010 [-0.0009,+0.0030]; p=0.20 -0.0057 [-0.0085,-0.0030]. The experiment's own pre-declared rule (exp25 docstring: break-even = smallest p whose CI includes or falls below zero) puts break-even at p=0.05, not "up to" 0.05. Only constrained average linkage (+0.0028 at 0.05, +0.0027 at 0.10) and re-apply stay positive. The non-monotone bump at 0.10 shows noise of about +-0.002. The wrong click is also the adversarial kind (merge of the two most similar clusters), so the threshold is specific to that error model.
Fix: "clearly beneficial up to about one wrong click in fifty (p=0.02), roughly neutral at one in twenty or ten, harmful at one in five, for look-alike wrong merges". Apply in abstract, Discussion, Conclusion. The review-and-undo recommendation survives.

**2. MAJOR - abstract over-generalises the "matches" claims.**
Quotes: "it matches average linkage without corrections to about one point"; "matches constrained average linkage in accuracy".
Evidence: Table I, CPLFW ours 0.841 vs 0.909 (6.9 points; text says 6.8 from rounded values; exp14_disjoint_edgeface_s.json whole_sets: 0.84055 vs 0.90914); occluded -0.9 points; lowres -0.6. Versus constrained average linkage (exp19, oracle user): all faces +0.0010 but on new faces -0.0014 [-0.0026,-0.0002] (casual -0.0017, error-prone -0.0057), and with the error-prone user all faces -0.0027. The body says "tied, not ours ahead" but the abstract says "matches".
Fix: "within one point of average linkage on simulated libraries (6.9 points lower on CPLFW) and within 0.003 F1 of constrained average linkage (lower on newly arrived faces)".

**3. MAJOR (selection and multiplicity) - "the more accurate method on clean, blurred and dark photos".**
Evidence: exp28 identity bootstrap, ours minus average linkage (coarse): clean +0.0059 [0.0020,0.0119]; blur +0.0038 [0.0024,0.0059]; dark +0.0022 [0.0016,0.0033]; jpeg -0.0005 [-0.0029,0.0017]; lowres -0.0061 [-0.0139,-0.0008]; mixed -0.0025 [-0.0060,0.0002]; occluded -0.0089 [-0.0219,0.0072]; lookalike +0.0037 [0.0000,0.0104]. Three favourable conditions of 7-8 are named in the abstract; the one significantly unfavourable one (lowres) is not. The intervals are 95% with no correction for 7-8 conditions x 8 baselines. By a normal approximation from the CI (clean SE about 0.0025, z about 2.3) the clean result would not survive a Bonferroni correction over 7 conditions; blur (z about 4) and dark (z about 5) would. All three sit at the F1 ceiling (0.992-0.998) with 0.2-0.6 point effects. The text gives no CI for blur and dark (they exist in the JSON).
Fix: report CIs for blur and dark, state "not corrected for multiple comparisons", and in the abstract say "slightly more accurate on clean, blurred and dark photos, less accurate on low-resolution photos". Add an overall (ALL) identity-bootstrap CI for ours vs average linkage (0.9854 vs 0.9857 is currently without interval).

**4. MAJOR - "All rules were fixed before any test number was computed" is not literally true.**
Location: Sec. IV, first sentence.
Evidence: exp28 docstring: fine grids, the 51-budget and the identity bootstrap "answer the external review of 2026-11-12", i.e. after exp14 test numbers had been seen; the wrong-click design (p=0.1, look-alike merges), the frozen baseline and the sweep rates were also added after earlier rounds' test results. exp16 (pose) was pre-declared and rejected, which is fine.
Fix: "Thresholds and recogniser were fixed on tuning libraries only; analyses added after review (fine grids, identity bootstrap, sweep, scaling, deletion) are marked as such."

**5. MINOR/MAJOR - consequence results are largely guaranteed by construction; one baseline is a strawman by design.**
Table III, Sec. VI-C. (a) "Retraction = never made 100%" for ours: exp22 compares (stored set minus one) with (same set, other arrival order). That is the Proposition applied; it tests order-invariance under constraints and nothing more. (b) The frozen baseline's retraction is an inverse "split by closeness to the two clicked faces", a naive inverse chosen by the authors; 5% (9%) shows how poor that inverse is, not what any product does. (c) Two devices, frozen: replicas also differ in the insertion order of faces (the frozen rule is greedy and order-dependent), so the 53% conflates edit-replay order with insertion order; exp21 mean ARI between replicas is 0.997 and F1 gap 0.0011, so "53% identical" overstates the practical gap. The paper discloses "our own design", but the abstract quotes 53% without it.
Fix: add "against a frozen-label baseline we designed" in the abstract; report mean ARI and F1 gap beside 53%; add a frozen variant with a canonical face order to separate the two causes; say in Discussion that the 100% rows follow from the Proposition.

**6. MINOR - the same timing differs across runs; the 16-69x ratios cannot be reproduced from printed numbers.**
Evidence: our read step at 8,290 faces, 0/10 corrections: exp20 0.51/1.86 s; exp24 0.32/1.28 s; exp27 0.60/1.97 s; exp29 0.44/1.25 s. The text prints 0.5/1.9 s (exp20) while "16 to 69 times at 8,290 faces" comes from exp29 (30.3/0.44 = 69; 19.6/1.25 = 15.7). From the printed 0.5/1.9 s a reader gets about 60x/10x. In exp29 the cached c-avg is faster with 10 corrections (19.6 s) than with none (30.3 s), while exp27 gives 32.1/31.3 s: run-to-run variance about 50%, single runs, no repeats. Sec. VII's "3.4-4.9 s" for the original merge mixes exp18 (3.38 s, last batch labels()) and exp20 (4.91 s). "Adding the faces and updating links took 0.15 s" is the last 829-face batch only (exp18 inc_add_s 0.145), but reads as the whole 8,290 faces (about 0.7 s summed).
Fix: re-time with 3-5 repeats on an idle machine and report medians; derive each ratio from the same run as the printed seconds; state that 0.15 s is per 829-face batch and that 3.4 and 4.9 s are two runs.

**7. MINOR - scaling claim: trend and comparator.**
Sec. VII. "This tests cost, not accuracy" is justified for what rotation can show (each copy keeps the per-copy component structure; no cross-copy edges), and the sentence concedes that real libraries differ in component sizes. Two things are missing. (i) Our read step grows superlinearly: exp29 0.44 s (8,290) to 15.4 s (49,740), 6x size for 35x time (exponent about 2), while the advantage over constrained average linkage shrinks from 69x to 15x (16x to 10.6x with corrections); a reader will extrapolate to parity near 100-200k. "An order of magnitude faster up to 50,000" is true, but the trend should be stated. (ii) The 9-15x range mixes the authors' cached NumPy constrained average linkage with unconstrained SciPy (8.7-11x), and both cluster faces, whereas ours clusters centroids after single linkage, so the gap is part algorithmic and part implementation. The 316 s index build (quadratic, about 6 ms per face) is 15-20x the read step and is outside the "faster" claim. The abstract should mark 50,000 as synthetic.
Fix: one sentence on the growth exponent and shrinking ratio; "synthetic" in the abstract.

**8. MINOR - exactness test wording (exp26 vs text).**
Quote: "identical partitions ... on all 240 test library-condition pairs ... after the real corrections of the three simulated users (up to ten each; they changed the result in 99% of the pairs that received any)".
Evidence: exp26_exact_binding_edgeface_s.json: libraries with at least one correction: oracle 213, casual 213, noisy 216 (MBF 210/210/214) of 240; identical in all (0 differences); partition changed in 213/213, 213/213, 215/216 (MBF 100%). So "99%" is conservative (pooled 641/642 = 99.8%), but "all 240 pairs" is 210-216 per user with corrections; the rest are covered by exp20's no-correction check (240/240). The 8,290-face check (exp20, identical at 0/10/50 corrections) used random corrections, which exp26's docstring notes rarely bind (per library 2.2 correct same-person, 4.0 cannot-link between random faces, 2.0 wrong). The reference under constraints is the dense full-matrix constrained merge (fast_merge=False), which is what is wanted.
Fix: "on all 240 pairs without corrections and on all 210-216 pairs per user that received corrections (all but one changed by them); the 8,290-face check used random corrections".

**9. MINOR - Poisson identity bootstrap (exp28), design details not in the paper.**
Sound in intent (keeps shared people shared). (i) Weights only re-weight per-face B-Cubed precision/recall computed from the unweighted clustering, so a zero-weight face still contributes to others' precision; this is a reweighting, not a re-run on a resampled population. (ii) Per-face scores are saved for shuffle order 0 only (`if sd == 0`), so for order-dependent baselines (First-Leader, Two-stage) the CI uses order 0 while the point estimate uses the 5-order mean; irrelevant for ours/average/single (deterministic). (iii) 1,571 is the number of identities appearing in test libraries (the half has 79 + 1,844 = 1,923). (iv) The ALL row has no bootstrap. (v) 1,000 resamples with percentile intervals is adequate.
Fix: one sentence on (i) and (iii); compute the ALL CI.

**10. MINOR - the fairer-tuning paragraph.**
(a) "no other baseline moved by more than 0.002": overall DBSCAN 0.9578 to 0.9599 and HDBSCAN 0.9773 to 0.9794 move +0.0021 (Single +0.0019). Per condition the fine grid does help some baselines: HDBSCAN lowres +0.006, occluded +0.011; Single occluded +0.036 (0.800 to 0.836), lowres -0.010. "Did not help them" holds for the strongest baseline and overall only. (b) "51 randomly chosen grid points" is 51 because the fine one-threshold grid has 51 points, while Sec. IV says one-threshold baselines have 11; state it. (c) The draws come from the same 360-point grid and three of five pick the full-grid winner or a neighbour; this shows low sensitivity to the budget, not that four parameters give no advantage. (d) The fine-grid comparison in the JSON gives mixed -0.0034 [-0.0071,-0.0002], which is significant against the fine-tuned baseline (coarse: not significant), and clean +0.0066 [0.0027,0.0128], lowres -0.0071 [-0.0144,-0.0017]; the text reports only the coarse-grid intervals.
Fix: "moved by about 0.002 or less overall"; add the mixed fine-grid result.

**11. MINOR - Proposition and rules versus code.**
"Corrections and deletion" matches `_labels_constrained` and `remove()`: must-links by decreasing cosine with content tie-break; core edges by decreasing similarity; attachment only for non-core faces whose group has no core, trying candidates in similarity order until one is allowed; centroid merge with a `bad` matrix; conflicts drop the less similar must-link; remove() recomputes core flags and union-find from the kept adjacency lists, drops and renumbers corrections; the unit test covers corrections on 140 synthetic points over 5 trials. The claim "two non-core faces joined by a must-link attach separately" is true (has_core is computed before attachment). Remarks: (a) core edges and attachment are tie-broken by face id (`(-e[0],e[1],e[2])`, `(-a[0],a[1])`, `(-c[0],c[1])`), unlike must-links; the proof calls this "exact ties", but near-ties through float32 FAISS inner products (query order differs for old/new pairs) and centroid sums (np.add.at in id order) can also flip a threshold decision; say "ties and floating-point rounding at a threshold". (b) Cluster order given to sklearn depends on root ids; this matters only for tied merges. (c) The deletion half of the Proposition is supported empirically by one 10% deletion on one clean 8,290-face library without corrections (exp30, one seed) plus the small synthetic unit test; "deleted photos are handled exactly" is a proof plus one run.
Fix: wording in the proof; add 5 random deletion seeds including corrections from exp19.
