## A. Findings (all MINOR; none changes a claim in the abstract or conclusion)

**F1 L192 "its F1 differs by up to 5.9 points between orders".** The 5.9 is the mean over 30 libraries of the max-min F1 across 5 orders in the worst condition (occluded 0.0587). The per-library maximum is 0.117 (exp14_testruns F1_spread). Read as "up to", it understates the worst case by half. Fix: "by 5.9 points on average in the worst condition (up to 11.7 on one library)".

**F2 L225 vs L168 (dark photos called "better" at +0.0022).** L168 defines a tie as "F1 differs by under 0.003 or the interval includes zero". +0.0022 is under 0.003, so by the paper's own rule dark is a tie. The identity CI is [0.0016,0.0033] (exp28), so the data would support "better", but the rule and the text disagree. Blur +0.0038 and clean +0.0059 are not affected. Fix: drop dark from the "better" list, or say "slightly better (interval excludes zero, difference under 0.003)".

**F3 L224-227 two conditions left out of the average-linkage comparison.** The text covers clean, blur, dark, low-res, JPEG and occluded, but not mixed (-0.0025, identity CI [-0.0060,+0.0002]) or look-alike (+0.0037, [0.0000,+0.0104]) (exp28). Their means are in the Table I caption, so nothing is hidden, but the reader has to find them. Under the library bootstrap (exp14_disjoint) mixed is a significant loss, [-0.0044,-0.0008]. Fix: add "mixed -0.0025 and look-alike +0.0037 (both intervals touch zero)".

**F4 L228 "most of the gain is the centroid merge".** From exp14_testruns, ours minus no-attach (attach effect) and no-attach minus single link (merge effect) give, by condition: clean +0.0111 and -0.0072, dark +0.0090 and -0.0050, look-alike +0.0056 and -0.0008, but occluded +0.0359 and +0.1073, low-res +0.0022 and +0.0144, mixed +0.0007 and +0.0218. On CALFW the merge alone is below single linkage (0.919 vs 0.945). The claim holds where the gain is large (occluded, low-res, mixed, CPLFW), not on clean, dark or look-alike photos. Fix: "the merge accounts for the large gains (occluded, low-res, mixed); on clean and dark photos it is the attach step".

**F5 L251-253 the c-avg lead on new photos is not a correction effect.** Verified: 0.0014 [0.0002,0.0026] (library-pair bootstrap; cluster-by-library [0.0004,0.0024]); casual -0.0017 and error-prone -0.0057, all CIs exclude zero. But with corrections discarded, c-avg is already ahead on new photos (0.9907 vs 0.9881, exp19 lost_new_F1). The gain from corrections on new photos is larger for ours (+0.0025 [0.0013,0.0040]) than for c-avg (+0.0013 [0.0008,0.0020]). The sentence is true but invites the reading "c-avg uses corrections better". Fix: add "(it is already ahead by 0.0026 without corrections)". Also L251 "within 0.001" is 0.00102 for the precise user; write "about 0.001".

**F6 L259-263 and L322 the sweep claim "clear up to about two wrong clicks in a hundred".** At p=0.02 the paper's interval is [+0.0015,+0.0054] (verified). The 240 pairs are 30 libraries x 8 degradations of the same photos, and the paper's caveat (L328) only mentions "79 shared people". Resampling whole libraries (my recomputation from exp25 per_library) gives p=0.02 [+0.0002,+0.0062], p=0.05 [-0.0016,+0.0032], p=0.20 [-0.0092,-0.0023]. The conclusions survive, but "clear" at p=0.02 is marginal. Re-apply at 0.20 [0.0010,0.0040] and c-avg at 0.10 [0.0016,0.0038] also survive. Fix: say "positive" for p=0.02, and state that the 8 conditions per library share photos so intervals are probably too narrow, as already done for FIW.

**F7 L176, L38-40, L263 scope of the "one wrong click in twenty" threshold.** The wrong click is always a same-person merge between the two most similar clusters whose majority people differ (exp19 wrong_merge). That is a hard kind of wrong click. Wrong "different people" clicks and random mis-clicks are not simulated. The abstract says "wrong click" without the qualifier. Fix: "wrong same-person clicks between look-alike people".

**F8 L134, L291-295, L38 device agreement assumes bit-identical embeddings.** The proposition says "exact arithmetic", and exp21 recomputes both replicas in one process from the same embeddings. Real devices may embed the same photo slightly differently (different ONNX provider or hardware), which can flip a pair near a threshold. The abstract's "identical people on two devices" holds by construction only for identical faces and embeddings. The abstract also gives only "53% and 31%", while the body reports mean ARI 0.997/0.996 and F1 gap 0.001-0.002 (verified), a milder story. Fix: add "given identical embeddings" in Sec. III or VI, and "(mean ARI 0.997)" next to 53% in the abstract.

**F9 L303 "all 1,372 held", L308-309 timings and deletion wording.** (a) "All held" is true by construction for consistent oracle corrections (held_share 1.0), so it is not an empirical result; say "as expected". (b) L309 "7-10 corrections that change the labels": exp30c made 7-10, but only 2-10 survived the deletion (30% rows keep 2-5), and "change the labels" is measured before the deletion. Fix: "7-10 corrections made (2-10 survive the deletion)". (c) Timings are single runs: the original read of the same 8,290 faces is 3.4 s (exp18) and 4.9 s (exp20), and 10 corrections (4.5 s) is faster than 0 (4.9 s). Ours at 0 corrections is 0.3-0.6 s across exp20/24/27/29. The orders of magnitude are safe; say "single runs, about +-30%".

Smaller notes (no fix needed): L193 "identical in every order and batch split" is backed in Table I by 3 orders of one batch size; the batch-split evidence is exp21 (different base and arrival batches, 240/240) and exp7 (earlier model). L214 CPLFW ours 0.841 is below Two-stage 0.897 and no-attach 0.892, which the text explains. For MBF the strongest baseline on occluded photos is HDBSCAN (0.963), not average linkage (0.954).

## C. Statistics notes
- Multiple comparisons: about 8 conditions x 7 baselines plus FIW, sweep and correction contrasts, with no correction (stated once, L224). The only "worse" call near the boundary is low-res (CI upper bound -0.0008); a correction would turn it into a tie, which is conservative for the authors' own method. The clean win (lower bound +0.0020) is the one a correction could weaken.
- Dependence: libraries share 79 people (stated), and the 8 degradation conditions share the same photos (not stated). Treat all pair-level intervals for corrections as optimistic (see F6).
- Tie rule: "difference < 0.003 OR interval includes zero" is an either/or rule, not an equivalence test. FIW "ties" (+0.0006 [-0.0046,+0.0061]) has an interval wider than the 0.003 margin, so equivalence is not shown; the text says "tied" per its own definition, which is fair but should not be read as equivalence.
- Single tuning/test split (seed 2026), no variability over splits. Disclosed ("split once").

## D. Devil's Advocate: strongest counter-argument
The paper's own sentence on L320-321 gives it away: the device agreement, exact retraction and exact deletion "follow from recomputing from the sets, so any such design shares them". Those are the headline robustness results, and they are close to identities of the design, measured against an edit-log baseline the authors built and say is not any product's sync. What is left empirically is small. Without corrections ours ties average linkage (0.9854 vs 0.9857) and loses 7 points on profile photos. The frozen-label rule, a few lines of code, has equal or higher F1 after the next import (0.9928 vs 0.9925, bold in Table II). Corrections add under one point of F1 from an oracle user, and the gain vanishes at one wrong click in twenty when the wrong clicks are look-alike merges. The clusterer is fast only against the authors' own constrained average linkage and a SciPy lower bound, on synthetic rotated copies, and the build step (316 s) dominates at 50,000 faces. Every user, device and wrong click is simulated, Immich is three easy libraries, and FIW is public figures. A reviewer who wants a deployable method would say the evidence supports "a reasonable engineering choice with exact semantics", not an accuracy or usability advantage.

What the paper honestly concedes (so this counter-argument is a limitation, not a flaw): the set-based properties are shared by any such design (L320-321); ours is not ahead on degraded photos and is worse on profile photos (L222-233, abstract); hard constraints are the least forgiving, so a review-and-undo view is needed and is untested (abstract, L263, L323); simulated users are upper bounds (L180); the device baseline is of their design (L187, L294); the Immich run says nothing about corrections, order or scale (L243); synthetic scale tests cost, not accuracy (L311-312); the lead narrows as libraries grow and the build dominates (L315-316); intervals on FIW and corrections are probably too narrow (L240, L267, L328); recogniser training data was not checked for LFW/FIW overlap (L163); no real albums, user study or tombstone test (L329-330).
