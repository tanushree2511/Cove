### Strongest Counter-Argument
Strip the framing and ask what a reader learns that was not obvious. If corrections are stored as face pairs and the result is recomputed from the set, the output cannot depend on arrival order, replicas that exchange the sets agree, and deleting a pair gives the never-made result. That is the definition of the design, not a finding, and the paper's own Corollary says any recompute-from-set method has it. Constrained average linkage does, sits within 0.0011 F1 of the proposed clusterer for correct clicks, and is ahead of it on new photos.

What is left is cost, and that is largely an implementation artefact. The 261 s and 293 s come from the authors' dense NumPy loop (1.1, 11 and 293 s at 1.7k, 3.3k and 8.3k faces: roughly cubic). A scipy version that gave identical partitions in seven checks took about 5 s on the same loaded laptop, about the same as the paper's own 4.7 s for re-clustering with the proposed method from scratch (measured before the fast read step).

The consequences section tests the proposed method against opponents the authors built. In my re-creation, replaying a merged event log converges every time and lands within 0.005 F1.

Finally, the thesis fails the paper's own sweep. At one wrong click in twenty the gain is statistically zero (+0.0009) while the simplest baseline, re-applying, keeps +0.0039, and the remedy depends on spotting the wrong click, which the paper's detector does at AUC 0.60. Everything runs on simulated users and libraries where F1 is already 0.985, so most comparisons are worth a face or two per library. The honest paper is an evaluation note, not a new method.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **The cost gap is cubic against quadratic.** The NumPy loop does an argmax over a dense matrix per merge; a compiled nearest-neighbour-chain implementation is quadratic. After that, 2 to 4 times remains at 8,290 faces. The one plausible real advantage (per-batch update, no dense matrix) was never measured.
2. **Convergence and undo come from a total order, not from a new clusterer.** Merge the two event logs by (time, device) and replay: both devices agree, and dropping an event gives exact retraction. Per-face last-writer-wins is the cheaper variant; it converges but holds fewer corrections (87.8%).
3. **The 47% retention is error overlap between two recognisers.** Corrections made against the old recogniser's mistakes help the new one only where it makes the same mistakes. A frozen store would keep every label through the upgrade for free.
4. **Hard against soft constraints.** Re-apply beats ours from 1 wrong click in 20 because it ignores about 30% of clicks, including wrong ones. A confidence-weighted or decaying constraint, which the paper names as the problem but does not try, is the obvious middle.
5. **"Fewer corrections needed" depends on where each method starts.** In E5, c-avg starts at F1 0.9862 against 0.9842 for ours (103 against 100 libraries below 0.99), so reaching 0.99 in fewer clicks (57% against 34% of libraries, median 3 against 4) mostly reflects the head start.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper treats the partition as the object the user cares about. A user cares about a named, persistent person. "Output depends only on the set, up to renaming of clusters" means cluster identities are not stable, so names, cover photos and hidden people need a matching step after every recompute (whose name survives a merge? who keeps the person after a split?). The current app overwrites user-assigned names, and corrections are keyed by face index. The measured stability (ARI of 0.998 or more between successive partitions) is on clean faces and counts faces, not named people. Identity continuity is the first thing a user of a "keeps your corrections" product would test, and it is not measured.

### Answers to the card
1. **Reason to exist.** The three consequences in V-D all follow from "recompute from the set". c-avg shares them (the paper concedes two; item iii follows by the same logic and E4 was not run for it). The remaining difference was cost; Check A puts it at 2 to 4 times at 8,290 faces and at parity up to about 3,000, so it is largely an implementation difference. What survives is a design principle plus a clean measurement of its price. See M1, M2.
2. **Effect sizes.** 0.001 F1 is less than one face per library; the whole benefit of corrections at p=0 is three to five faces; the benchmark is saturated (F1 0.985 before corrections). Fine for an honest tie paper; unsupportable for any "better by 0.002" sentence. See m3.
3. **Frozen + replayed edits.** Not a straw in intent (Immich's rule plus the textbook way to combine edit logs) but a weak design in effect: divergence and inexact undo come from not syncing labels and not replaying a merged log. "Pairwise facts plus recompute" is our method; per-face last-writer-wins converges but is worse (88% held, F1 0.980). See M2.
4. **Simulated users.** Field-standard, but the decisive parameter is assumed. See m6.
5. **Retention 47%.** A ratio of two significant but tiny gains with a wide interval, no comparator, and mostly error overlap. See m4.
6. **Thesis or list of experiments.** "Corrections stay put" is true by construction; the benefit part of the thesis holds only below about 1 wrong click in 20 to 50; the second half of the paper reads as a list. See M3, m7.
